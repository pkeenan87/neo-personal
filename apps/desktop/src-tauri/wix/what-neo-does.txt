What Neo does

Neo protects this computer from remote-access scams. In a typical scam, someone calls you, gets you to install a program such as AnyDesk or to open Quick Assist, and then takes control of the computer.

Neo watches for that and does two things:
  1. It tells the person at the keyboard, at once and in plain words.
  2. It tells the household owner who set Neo up, by email, within a minute.

What Neo checks on this computer: the programs that are installed, the programs that are running, the services that are installed, and the log files that remote-access tools write when someone connects.

What Neo sends: only the name of a remote-access tool or flagged program when one appears, a remote peer ID during an incoming session, and the fingerprint (SHA-256) of an unsigned new program so it can be checked. Neo never sends the list of your programs, your files or your browsing.

How it works: a Windows service called "Neo Protection" runs in the background and holds the sign-in token for this computer. The Neo icon next to the clock shows that protection is on. You can stop protection from that icon; the household owner will be told.

Neo does not block, remove or close anything. It warns.
