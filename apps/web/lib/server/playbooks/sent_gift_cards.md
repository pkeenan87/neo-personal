# Playbook: sent_gift_cards — "I bought gift cards for someone"

Use when the person bought gift cards and shared the card numbers or photos of them, often because someone claimed to be a boss, relative, government agency, tech support, or a romantic interest. Ask which brand of card and whether the numbers have already been shared. For money cases, suggest involving a trusted person.

## First, right now
1. Stop: do not buy any more cards and do not send any more numbers, whatever the person on the phone or in the chat says.
2. Keep the cards and every receipt. Take photos of the back of each card and the receipt.
3. Contact the card issuer immediately using the support page on its official website or the number printed on the back of the card (not a number the scammer gave). Tell them the card was used in a scam and ask them to freeze any remaining balance.
4. Stop talking to the scammer. Block the number, email, or account.

## Then
- Issuer support (use the official site, then its fraud or gift card scam page):
  - Apple gift cards: support.apple.com (search "gift card scams").
  - Google Play: support.google.com/googleplay.
  - Amazon: amazon.com customer service, gift card section.
  - Target and Walmart: the customer service pages on target.com and walmart.com.
  - Steam: help.steampowered.com.
- If they paid with a credit or debit card, call the number on the back of that card and explain it was a scam purchase; ask what can be done.
- Go back to the store where the cards were bought if it is nearby; some stores can contact the issuer faster.
- Write down what happened while it is fresh: dates, names used, numbers and accounts the scammer used, amounts.

## Report
- FTC: https://reportfraud.ftc.gov, or call 1-877-382-4357 (US).
- IC3 (FBI internet crime): https://www.ic3.gov, especially if the loss is large.
- The scam text that started it: forward it to 7726.
- Outside the US: their country's consumer protection agency (UK: Action Fraud; Canada: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre; Australia: Scamwatch).

## What to watch for next
- "Recovery" offers: anyone who contacts them promising to get the money back for a fee is running a second scam.
- The same scammer returning with a new story ("there's a problem with the cards, buy more to fix it").
- Calls from someone claiming to be the FTC, police, or the card company asking for more money or codes. Real agencies do not ask for gift cards.

## Reassurance
Gift card scams are designed to rush smart, kind people, and they fool a great many every day. Reporting quickly gives the issuer the best chance to freeze what is left, and the report helps stop the same scammer from reaching others.
