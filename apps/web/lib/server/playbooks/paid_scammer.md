# Playbook: paid_scammer — "I sent money"

Use when the person sent money to someone who may be a scammer: bank wire, Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, PayPal, cryptocurrency (including a crypto ATM), or cash (mailed or handed to a courier). Ask how they paid and how long ago; speed matters most for wires and card payments. For money cases, suggest involving a trusted person.

## First, right now
1. Stop sending. Do not send more, even if told it will "unlock" or "refund" the first payment.
2. Call your bank or card issuer using the number on the back of your card (or inside the official app). Say "I was scammed and need to report fraud" and ask them to stop or recall the payment.
3. For payment apps (Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, PayPal), report the payment inside the app and also call your bank, since Zelle runs through it.
4. Stop contact with the scammer and save everything: messages, phone numbers, account details, receipts, transaction ids.

## Then
- Wire transfers: ask the bank to send a recall request to the receiving bank today. The sooner, the better the chance.
- Card payments: ask about a chargeback or dispute.
- Crypto: contact the exchange or crypto ATM operator through its official website with the transaction id and wallet address. Recovery is hard, so report quickly.
- Cash sent by mail or courier: if mailed, contact USPS at 1-800-275-8777 (or the shipping company's official support) with the tracking number; they may be able to intercept it.
- If they shared bank log-in details, change them now and follow entered_password. If the scammer had remote access, follow device_compromised.
- Consider a free credit freeze with the three US credit bureaus if they shared a Social Security number or ID documents.

## Report
- FTC: https://reportfraud.ftc.gov, or 1-877-382-4357 (US).
- IC3 (FBI internet crime): https://www.ic3.gov. Include transaction ids and wallet addresses.
- Local police for a report number, if the bank asks for one.
- Outside the US: their country's consumer protection agency (UK: Action Fraud; Canada: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre; Australia: Scamwatch; EU: national police cybercrime service).

## What to watch for next
- "Recovery" scams: someone claiming to be a lawyer, government agency, or investigator who can recover the money for a fee. Real agencies never charge to recover funds.
- A "refund department" reaching out to say they sent too much back: this is the classic next step.
- Unexpected accounts or loans in their name if personal details were shared.

## Reassurance
Scammers are professionals who use urgency and fear on purpose; this happens to careful, intelligent people every day. Calling the bank quickly and reporting gives the best chance of stopping the payment and protects others too.
