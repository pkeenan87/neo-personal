# Playbook: device_compromised — "Someone had remote access to my device"

Use when a "tech support", bank, or refund caller got the person to install a remote-access app (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, UltraViewer, Quick Assist, or similar) or to type a code that let them control the computer or phone. Ask whether they logged into any bank or email account while the caller was connected, and whether money moved.

## First, right now
1. Disconnect: end the call, then turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the network cable so the remote session stops.
2. Uninstall the remote-access app (on Windows: Settings → Apps; on a Mac: drag it from Applications to the Bin; on a phone: delete the app).
3. From a different, trusted device, change the passwords for email and banking, and sign out other sessions.
4. Call your bank using the number on the back of your card if they logged into banking during the call or shared card details.

## Then
- Run a full scan: Microsoft Defender on Windows (Windows Security → Virus & threat protection → Scan options → Full scan). On a Mac, keep macOS updated and remove unknown apps and login items (System Settings → General → Login Items).
- Check for new user accounts, browser extensions, and apps installed during the call; remove them.
- If you are unsure the device is clean, ask a trusted technician or back up your files and reset the device.
- Check email for forwarding rules and recovery options you did not add:
  - Google: https://myaccount.google.com/security-checkup.
  - Apple: https://account.apple.com.
  - Microsoft: https://account.live.com/Activity.
- Turn on two-step verification on email and banking.

## Report
- FTC: https://reportfraud.ftc.gov (US), or 1-877-382-4357.
- IC3: https://www.ic3.gov, especially if money moved.
- If the caller claimed to be Microsoft or Apple, those companies accept scam reports through their official support websites.
- Outside the US: their country's consumer protection agency.

## What to watch for next
- The same people calling back ("we saw a problem", "we refunded too much"). Hang up; real companies do not make unsolicited tech-support calls.
- Pop-ups with a phone number to call. Close the browser; never call numbers from pop-ups.
- Unexpected transfers or new payees in online banking over the coming weeks.

## Reassurance
Cutting the connection and removing the app stops their access. Changing passwords from a clean device closes the doors they may have seen. Many people are caught by these calls because they sound so official; asking for help now is exactly right.
