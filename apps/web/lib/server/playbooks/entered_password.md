# Playbook: entered_password — "I typed my password somewhere"

Use when the person typed a password (and maybe a code) into a page that may be fake. Ask which account it was for, and whether they use that same password anywhere else. Those two answers decide the order below.

## First, right now
1. Go to the real site or app yourself (type the address or use the app, not the link) and change that password. Make it new and unique.
2. If the same password is used on other accounts, change it there too, starting with email, then banking, then shopping and social media.
3. Sign out other sessions: most services have "Sign out of all other devices" next to the password setting.
4. Turn on two-step verification (an authenticator app or passkey is best; text codes are better than nothing).

## Then
- Email account first: whoever controls your email can reset everything else. Check for forwarding rules, filters, and recovery addresses you did not add, and remove them.
  - Google: Security Checkup at https://myaccount.google.com/security-checkup, and Gmail Settings → Forwarding and filters.
  - Apple: review devices and trusted phone numbers at https://account.apple.com.
  - Microsoft: recent activity at https://account.live.com/Activity, then Outlook Settings → Mail → Forwarding and Rules.
- Check recent sign-ins and connected apps on the affected account and remove anything unfamiliar.
- If it was a bank or card login: call the number on the back of your card and tell them. Ask them to watch for unusual activity and whether a new card or account number makes sense.
- Consider a password manager so every account gets its own password; it also refuses to fill passwords on fake sites.
- If they also entered a one-time code, follow shared_code as well.

## Report
- Phishing email: "Report phishing" in Gmail or Outlook; Apple-themed scams to reportphishing@apple.com.
- Phishing text: forward it to 7726.
- The fake page: https://safebrowsing.google.com/safebrowsing/report_phish/.
- If money or identity is involved: https://reportfraud.ftc.gov (US), or IC3 at https://www.ic3.gov for internet crime. Outside the US, their country's consumer protection agency.

## What to watch for next
- Password-reset emails they did not ask for, and sign-in alerts from new places. Check them inside the real app.
- Messages to their contacts from their account asking for money or gift cards: warn friends and family if the account was misused.
- A "security team" or "refund department" calling to help: that is the next step of the same scam.

## Reassurance
A typed password is recoverable in minutes: once it is changed and other sessions are signed out, the old one is useless to whoever has it. Acting within the hour, as they are now, is what matters most.
